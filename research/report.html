Therapist website research

Research and design direction · Vancouver counselling practice · 14 November 2026

The recommendation

Build a small, exceptional information website that helps visitors answer four questions quickly: Is this person a possible fit for me? What would working together be like? Can I practically access the service? How do I make contact? Distinctive typography, composition and artwork can make the practice memorable; clear information and a real human presence must carry the decision.

01 Open at Any Page is the lead direction. Its four olive question tabs help visitors arriving from the clinic profile find the information they came for. 02 A Personal Introduction uses a first-person letter and real portrait to make the handoff feel personal. Four further olive directions explore more expressive interactions; eight additional directions remain available under Exploratory themes.

The scope is informational: Home, About & Approach, Services, Fees & Questions, Contact. No calendar, accounts, newsletter, booking workflow or intake form is needed. Email and phone are sufficient when the real contact details are approved. Until then, all names, credentials, services, fees, addresses, availability and photographs of people or premises must remain clearly identified placeholders.

What the evidence supports

First impressions respond to visual order. Tuch and colleagues tested website screenshots at very short exposures. Lower visual complexity and more familiar-looking website structures generally received higher aesthetic ratings. This supports one strong focal point, clear grouping and recognizable navigation. It does not establish that a minimalist therapist website produces more appointments, or that visitors make their entire clinical choice in milliseconds. The research was published in 2012 and measured screenshot judgments, not contemporary mobile therapy inquiries. Primary study, Google Research

Attractiveness does not replace content. Thielsch, Blotenberg and Jaron studied perceived content, usability and aesthetics across three studies. Aesthetics mattered to first impressions; content was important across the phases studied, including intentions to return or recommend. This supports giving the homepage a memorable opening while making qualifications, approach and practical information easy to inspect. Their findings concern perceptions and stated intentions across general websites, not demonstrated therapy conversion. Primary study, Interacting with Computers

There is no universally optimal aesthetic. Reinecke and colleagues collected ratings for 450 websites from 548 volunteers and modelled how complexity, colourfulness and demographic variables relate to appeal. The work supports testing designs with the intended audience rather than presenting a particular colour as scientifically calming for everyone. It does not provide a universal palette prescription for therapists. Primary study, Harvard repository

Credibility requires substance behind the presentation. Stanford’s research-based credibility guidelines recommend verifiable information, visible expertise, real people, clear contact information and professional presentation. For this practice, the reasonable application is an authentic portrait, accurately described qualifications, a link to the relevant professional register if appropriate, a plain-language approach and verified location details. These older general-web guidelines are useful principles, not a modern clinical conversion experiment. Stanford Web Credibility Guidelines

Design inference for this project: use expressive art direction with a familiar information structure. A poetic headline needs a plain-language subtitle saying who the therapist is, what they offer and where. Keep contact visible without flashing, countdowns, pop-ups or claims of guaranteed transformation. No source reviewed establishes that any of these fourteen concepts will outperform the others; that requires user testing and, after launch, appropriately measured real inquiries.

Content that should earn its place

A good homepage sequence is: a clear welcome and contact action; a concise explanation of who the practice helps; a real introduction to the clinician; what working together involves; fees and practical details; common questions; contact. Individual concepts can reorder these to suit their visual storytelling, while preserving quick access to essentials.

The About page should distinguish the therapist’s identity, qualifications and working style. Translate professional modalities into what a visitor can reasonably expect, with the clinician reviewing the wording. Services should describe only confirmed populations and concerns. Fees should show the actual rate, session length and applicable policies when approved. Contact should include the verified address or service area, phone/email, access information and truthful response expectations. These are recommendations for reducing unanswered questions; they are not clinical advice or promises about outcomes.

Use a real portrait and real practice images when available. Until then, a clearly marked portrait placeholder is more honest than a stock person presented as the therapist. Abstract artwork can establish character, but should not carry essential information. All illustrative sample copy needs clinician review before publication.

Search visibility: what actually matters

Visibility is whether relevant people see the practice in search. Engagement describes what visitors do on the site. Conversion is a meaningful next step, such as an inquiry; clicking an email link is only an intent signal, not proof that an inquiry was sent or a session arranged. A tool’s “SEO score” is a diagnostic checklist, not a forecast of traffic, clinical fit or revenue.

Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence/popularity. Complete, accurate Business Profile details help Google understand the practice; physical distance is not something visual design can fix. If eligible, verify and maintain the actual business profile, address, category, hours and photographs. Keep information consistent with the website and legitimate professional listings. Google Business Profile local ranking guidance

For the website, use descriptive page titles, helpful original text, crawlable links and sensible page organization. Write naturally about the actual service and Vancouver location; do not repeat city names mechanically. A suitable title pattern is “[Verified service] in Vancouver | [Therapist name]”. Give genuinely distinct confirmed services their own useful pages when warranted. Do not create near-identical pages for every neighbourhood. Google explicitly offers no automatic first-place ranking formula; changes may take weeks or longer to evaluate. Google SEO Starter Guide

Performance should support the design. Compress and size images appropriately, keep text as real text, reserve image space to prevent layout jumps and avoid heavyweight video backgrounds. Google’s Core Web Vitals guidance targets LCP within 2.5 seconds, INP below 200 ms and CLS below 0.1. These are implementation targets to verify on the real site; the concepts have not earned live performance scores. Google Core Web Vitals guidance

Accurate LocalBusiness structured data can describe a business to search engines. Select a type appropriate to the verified practice, include only true public information and validate it. Structured data does not guarantee enhanced search results. Do not mark up invented reviews, prices, credentials or medical classifications. Google LocalBusiness documentation

Google discusses reviews as a local signal, but general business advice is not a sufficient basis for a therapist testimonial strategy. Before any review solicitation or testimonial use, the clinician should confirm their applicable professional obligations and confidentiality approach. This project uses none. Legitimate professional-directory profiles, accurate information and useful content are safer starting tasks than building a campaign around patient endorsements.

After launch, use Search Console to observe indexing, relevant queries, impressions and clicks. Review inquiry quality with the clinician without recording clinical details in analytics. If analytics are added, measure contact-link use separately from actual inquiries and sessions. Compare trends rather than promising a visitor count before the practice’s specialties, competition, existing domain and search demand are known.

Accessibility and responsive design

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA in implementation. Normal text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast; large text needs at least 3:1. Muted palettes still require readable text. W3C contrast guidance

WCAG 2.2’s minimum pointer target criterion is 24 by 24 CSS pixels, with stated exceptions including spacing. For the primary mobile actions, a more generous approximately 44-pixel target is a practical design choice. W3C target-size guidance

Also verify keyboard operation, visible focus, heading order, link names, alternative text, text enlargement and responsive reflow. Essential information must not depend on hover, colour or animation. Use a linear mobile reading order; avoid sideways scrolling for ordinary content. Any optional motion should be restrained and respect reduced-motion preferences. These are launch checks, not a claim that an exploratory mockup is certified compliant. WCAG 2.2

What to confirm before selecting and publishing

Confirm the clinician’s public name, exact professional designation, registration details, services, intended clients, modalities, location, in-person/online scope, fees, session lengths, contact channels, availability, accessibility information and image permissions. Confirm whether the name the project owner belongs to the therapist or is only the project recipient. Do not turn it into a practice identity without confirmation.

Choose three concepts for a brief review with representative prospective users: can they identify the service and location, explain the approach, find fees, and locate contact without coaching? Ask what feels trustworthy, unclear or uncomfortable. Revise based on observed difficulty rather than asking only which design looks nicest. This is a recommended evaluation method, not research already performed.

Before launch: replace every placeholder; obtain clinician approval of claims; verify links and contact details; test desktop/mobile and keyboard use; check contrast and zoom; compress assets; verify titles, indexability, sitemap and structured data; configure Search Console; review privacy and professional requirements relevant to the actual practice. The final website should make a strong impression and then make choosing a next step straightforward.

The Open Book pairs the portrait with direct questions visitors can explore. The first two olive concepts now give the portrait more space and strengthen their opening typography.

StringTune-inspired interaction study

A visual review of StringTune by Fiddle.Digital showed an unusually cohesive experience: a dark opening dominated by oversized lettering and detailed artwork, a dramatic animated object moving through the composition, sticky scenes, changes of scale, and a recurring character. Its technical description also highlights cursor tracking and scroll progress. The useful lesson is to design the whole sequence around a visual world; attaching a small animation to an ordinary layout does not create the same effect.
